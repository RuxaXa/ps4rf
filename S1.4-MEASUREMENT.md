# S1.4 — measurement on FW 13.52

Image: PS4 `kernel.bin`, SHA-256 `cf157ffd…` (NOT_PRISTINE: 26 GoldHEN/afterglow kpatch sites
are already written into it — recorded in `facts.image.purity_evidence`, and a caveat for every
number below). Graph: finding-centred 96/2, 1814 nodes, 11359 edges. Scan artefact: 2053
scanned functions, 608 findings, 395 of them at rank ≤ 2 (the primitive set).

The binding constraint, straight out of `counts.next_obligation`:

* **211** chains stopped at `REQ_UNPRIV_ENTRY` (rung 2),
* **159** stopped at `REQ_ENTRY_REACHABLE` (no entry-rooted path at all).

Not one chain advanced past rung 1. That is the S1.4 result in a single line: the engine can
prove reachability, and it cannot yet prove that anybody unprivileged gets there.

## P4.2 — what was actually closed

**Dispatch (closed, mechanically).** 687 syscall slots resolved from `sysent`
(`rva 0x1102b70`, stride 48, `sy_call` at +8), each recorded with the slot address and the
pointer that was read, e.g. `sysent[0] → slot 0x1102b70, pointer field 0x1102b78, handler
0x1c0280` after VA→RVA conversion through the image base `0xffffffffd00d0000`.

**Object identity (closed for 46 findings / 35 distinct functions).** Alias-tracked, and backed
by field offsets rather than by a register name — one example verbatim:

> every site uses an alias of `rax` (`r12`, `r14`, `rax`); the same field offsets `[0, 24, 32]`
> are written on that base, so the sites mean the same object

57 findings additionally carry shared field offsets. This is the consumer the repaired store
summary never had.

**Authorization: nothing closed.** All 24 sampled entries returned `UNKNOWN`. The reason is not
the walk, it is the input:

> authorization symbols are not resolved in this firmware (`priv_check_cred`, `priv_check`,
> `kauth_cred_get`, `kauth_cred_issuser`, `suser`, `p_cansee`, `cr_cansee`); without at least
> one of them, or a device-record mode, the absence of a credential gate cannot be shown from
> the image

Zero credential gates were detected in the sampled walks, and a `prison0` reference would have
been reported as positive evidence if one had been present.

## P4.3 — missing requirements and their technical cause

| requirement | chains/findings affected | technical cause |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `REQ_UNPRIV_ENTRY` | 211 chains | anchor set has no authorization symbol; `cdevsw` facts carry no mode field |
| `REQ_ENTRY_REACHABLE` | 159 chains | no entry-rooted path in the finding-centred graph (the walk is bounded at 96 functions / depth 2) |
| `REQ_OBJECT_FLOW` (no drop site) | 306 findings | the finding's function contains no refcount-drop site — the detector's primitive is not a release, so there is no identity question to answer |
| `REQ_OBJECT_FLOW` (alias refused) | 43 findings | the sites act on different registers and no bounded alias chain connects them; refused, not assumed |
| `REQ_CHAIN_STABILITY` | all | needs a measurement on hardware; unreachable offline by construction |

Sampling limit, stated: 687 dispatches were resolved but only **24** were walked for gates
(depth 2, budget 24 functions each). A 192-entry sweep did not finish in fifteen minutes. The
sample is of entries, not of the analysis.

## P4.4 — false positives and refused negatives

* **No chain was upgraded.** Nothing sits above `CFG_REACHABLE`; the ladder did not promote a
  single proven path, and `next_obligation` names the missing requirement for every one.
* **43 identity claims were refused** with the site and the offending register
  (`not every site acts on an alias of r12: [{'site': '0x347620'…`), and 306 were refused for
  having no drop site at all. Refusals are the fail-closed direction and they dominate: 349 of
  395 findings were refused rather than proved.
* **Two silent-wrong-answer bugs were caught by running the measurement, not by reading code:**
  1. the artefact published `dataclasses.asdict(chain)`, which drops the computed
     `next_obligation` — every chain then looked as if no obligation were open;
  2. object flow analysed with a release anchor guessed from the finding text, so all 395
     findings reported "no refcount-drop site found" — a confident false negative.
  Both are fixed and pinned by tests.
* **A third defect was caught in my own first result and voided:** the initial alias closure was
  transitive over every `mov`, producing an 11-register "alias set" and 22 "shared field
  offsets" (including 0, 8, 12 …) — i.e. memory the function touched, not a field of one
  object. That run reported 68 proved identities and was withdrawn; the tightened rule (three
  hops, clobber kills the alias, interior pointers kept, field writes only on the sites' own
  bases) gives 46.

## P4.5 — which historical root causes this reconstructs

**None of the three named families on this image.** Lapse is fixed in 12.50, netcontrol in
13.02 (PS4), and Relapse is a PS5 case fixed in 14.00 — all outside FW 13.52. Using them as
positives here would produce a false negative that measures the firmware choice, not the
engine. Details, and why even the negative control is currently undecidable without symbols, are
in `S1.4-RETRO-VALIDATION.md`.

What *is* reconstructible with today's engine: reachability to a function from a resolved
dispatch slot, and object identity inside a function with field-level evidence. What is not:
unprivileged reachability, multi-hop ownership, and any statement about exploitability.

## The next two things, in order

1. Resolve authorization symbols, or add a `cdevsw` mode table. Rung 2 is otherwise unreachable
   for every chain, and every object-flow result stays unusable.
2. Get an image inside the affected range of a chosen family (or a holdback family whose range
   covers 13.52) before attempting any historical rediscovery claim.
